# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** SOP-IC-003  
**Title:** Central Line-Associated Bloodstream Infection (CLABSI) Prevention Protocol  
**Domain:** Infection Control  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) defines evidence-based infection prevention protocols for insertion, daily maintenance, and prompt removal of Central Venous Catheters (CVC) to eliminate preventable Central Line-Associated Bloodstream Infections (CLABSI).

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all healthcare providers who insert, manage, or access central line catheters, including ICU physicians, vascular access specialists, critical care nurses, and emergency department staff.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Vascular Access Team Nurse / ICU Lead Nurse** is responsible for conducting daily line necessity audits, maintaining sterile dressing integrity, and monitoring line site care.

### Clause 2.2: Insertion Supervision
The **Attending Physician or Certified Vascular Specialist** is responsible for maintaining full barrier precautions during central line insertion and completing the CVC Insertion Checklist.

---

## Section 3: Central Line Insertion Bundle

### Clause 3.1: Site Selection and Skin Preparation
1. Avoid the femoral vein for CVC placement in adult patients; prefer the subclavian or internal jugular sites.
2. Perform skin antisepsis using a >0.5% chlorhexidine gluconate solution with 70% isopropyl alcohol.
3. Allow the skin antiseptic to air dry completely (at least 2 minutes) prior to skin puncture.

### Clause 3.2: Maximal Sterile Barrier Precautions
During insertion, the operator and all assisting personnel must use maximal sterile barrier precautions: cap, mask, sterile gown, sterile gloves, and a full-body sterile drape covering the patient.

---

## Section 4: Maintenance and Line Access Protocol

### Clause 4.1: Scrubbing the Hub
Before accessing any needleless connector or injection port, personnel must execute the following sequential scrubbing steps:
1. Perform hand hygiene and wear clean gloves.
2. Scrub the needleless connector vigorously with an 70% alcohol swab or chlorhexidine-alcohol pad for a minimum of **15 seconds**.
3. Allow the hub to air dry completely for **15 seconds** prior to attaching a syringe or IV tubing.

### Clause 4.2: Dressing Changes and Site Care
1. Replace transparent semi-permeable CVC dressings every **7 days**, or immediately if the dressing becomes damp, loose, or visibly soiled.
2. Replace gauze dressings every **48 hours**.
3. Use a chlorhexidine-impregnated sponge/dressing over the CVC insertion site for all adult patients.

### Clause 4.3: Tubing and Connector Replacement
1. Replace continuous IV administration sets not containing lipids, blood, or blood products no more frequently than at **96-hour intervals**, but at least every 7 days.
2. Replace tubing used for blood products, lipid emulsions, or propofol within **24 hours** of initiating infusion.

---

## Section 5: Daily Assessment and Prompt Removal

### Clause 5.1: Daily Line Review
The clinical team must evaluate CVC necessity during daily interdisciplinary rounds using the "Daily CVC Checklist".

### Clause 5.2: Immediate Removal Criteria
The central line must be removed immediately if any of the following conditions are met:
1. The catheter is no longer clinically necessary.
2. Signs of localized infection (purulence, severe erythema, induration) are observed at the insertion site.
3. Unexplained systemic bacteremia or sepsis is suspected.
