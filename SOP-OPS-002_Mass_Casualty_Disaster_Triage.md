# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** SOP-OPS-002  
**Title:** Mass Casualty Incident (MCI) and Disaster Triage Protocol  
**Domain:** Hospital Operations  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) defines disaster triage operational structures, surge capacity expansion protocols, and patient categorization models during external or internal Mass Casualty Incidents (MCI).

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all emergency department physicians, trauma surgeons, disaster response teams, triage nurses, security staff, and hospital administrative leadership.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Emergency Department Triage Officer (Attending Physician or Senior ER Nurse)** is responsible for performing rapid triage categorizations at the intake decontamination zone and directing patient flow.

### Clause 2.2: Incident Command Lead
The **Hospital Incident Commander (Chief Medical Officer / ED Director)** is responsible for activating the Hospital Emergency Operations Center (HEOC) and declaring internal disaster response levels.

---

## Section 3: START Triage Categorization System

### Clause 3.1: Triage Methodology Standard
Staff must utilize the **Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment (START)** protocol for adult patients (or JumpSTART for pediatric victims) to categorize casualties within **60 seconds per patient**.

### Clause 3.2: Triage Color Classifications
1. **RED Tag (Immediate):** Severe life-threatening injuries requiring immediate intervention within 15–30 minutes (e.g., airway compromise, tension pneumothorax, uncontrolled severe hemorrhage).
2. **YELLOW Tag (Delayed):** Serious injuries requiring medical care within 1 to 2 hours, but stable without immediate life threat (e.g., non-compromised open fractures, controlled soft tissue trauma).
3. **GREEN Tag (Minor / Walking Wounded):** Minor injuries where care can be safely deferred for several hours; patients can ambulate independently.
4. **BLACK Tag (Expectant / Deceased):** Deceased victims or catastrophic injuries where survival is unlikely even with maximal intervention (e.g., massive brain tissue destruction, absent pulse/spontaneous respirations after airway opening).

---

## Section 4: MCI Activation and Intake Workflow

### Clause 4.1: Operational Activation Sequence
Upon notification of an incoming mass casualty event from local Emergency Medical Services (EMS), staff must execute the following sequential workflow:
1. The ED Attending Physician notifies the Hospital Administrator on Call and declares a "Code Yellow (MCI Activation)".
2. Activate the Hospital Emergency Operations Center (HEOC) and assign Incident Command roles.
3. Establish external triage posts outside the ambulance bay entrance to screen incoming arrivals.
4. Convert the Emergency Department treatment bays to RED category resuscitation zones.
5. Re-allocate stable inpatient beds by expediting discharge of low-acuity ward patients to holding areas.
6. Station Triage Nurses at intake gates to apply color-coded triage tags and wristbands to all incoming casualties prior to hospital entry.

---

## Section 5: De-escalation and Incident Reporting

### Clause 5.1: Incident Termination Criteria
The Hospital Incident Commander authorizes termination of MCI status once all incoming casualties have been triaged, stabilized, and assigned to definitive treatment areas or surgical suites.

### Clause 5.2: Post-Disaster Debriefing
Within **48 hours** of disaster de-activation, the Disaster Management Committee must convene a formal hot-debriefing session and submit an After-Action Report (AAR) to the Board of Directors.
