# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** SOP-IC-005  
**Title:** Sterilization and Decontamination of Surgical Instruments  
**Domain:** Infection Control  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) specifies mandatory requirements for the cleaning, decontamination, packaging, sterilization, and storage of reusable medical and surgical instruments to prevent healthcare-associated infections and ensure surgical safety.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all Central Sterile Supply Department (CSSD) technicians, operating room scrub nurses, endoscopic processing staff, and surgical service personnel across all hospital facilities.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **CSSD Supervisor** is responsible for overseeing daily sterilization operations, verifying biological indicator test results, and approving sterile load releases.

### Clause 2.2: Technician Execution
The **Central Processing Technician** is responsible for manually pre-cleaning, loading washer-disinfectors, wrapping instrument trays, and operating steam autoclaves according to verified parameters.

---

## Section 3: Decontamination and Cleaning Workflow

### Clause 3.1: Point-of-Use Pre-Treatment
Immediately following a procedure, surgical personnel must wipe gross soil from instruments using a damp sterile sponge and spray instruments with an enzymatic pre-cleaner to prevent drying of organic matter.

### Clause 3.2: Manual and Automated Cleaning Sequence
When processing soiled instruments in the CSSD decontamination area, technicians must execute the following sequential steps:
1. Don full decontamination PPE (heavy-duty nitrile gloves, fluid-resistant gown, face shield, and protective footwear).
2. Submerge instruments in an enzymatic detergent solution prepared at the manufacturer’s specified concentration and temperature.
3. Clean all surfaces, hinges, and lumens using soft-bristled brushes while fully submerged under water to prevent aerosol generation.
4. Rinse thoroughly with critical (purified) water to remove detergent residues.
5. Load instruments into the automated ultrasonic cleaner or washer-disinfector for full thermal disinfection cycles.

---

## Section 4: Packaging and Sterilization Parameters

### Clause 4.1: Inspection and Packaging
1. Inspect cleaned instruments under magnification for cleanliness, alignment, and functionality.
2. Wrap instrument trays using double-layer non-woven polypropylene wrap or rigid sterilization containers.
3. Place a chemical indicator strip inside every package and apply external indicator tape to each wrapped tray.

### Clause 4.2: Steam Autoclave Parameters
Dynamic Air Removal (Pre-vacuum) steam sterilizers must maintain the following minimum validated exposure parameters:
1. Temperature: **132°C (270°F)** for a minimum exposure time of **4 minutes** at 27 to 30 psi.
2. Alternative Temperature: **135°C (275°F)** for a minimum exposure time of **3 minutes**.
3. Minimum drying time: **20 to 30 minutes** post-sterilization cycle.

---

## Section 5: Sterility Quality Control & Load Release

### Clause 5.1: Quality Monitoring Frequency
1. Chemical Indicators: Verify color change on external tape and internal indicator before releasing any pack.
2. Biological Indicators: Perform a Biological Indicator (Geobacillus stearothermophilus) test at least daily for each autoclave, and in **every load containing implantable devices**.

### Clause 5.2: Load Recall Protocol
If a biological indicator yields a positive result (indicating failure), the CSSD Supervisor must immediately halt autoclave operations, quarantine the unit, and recall all items processed in that autoclave since the last negative biological test.
