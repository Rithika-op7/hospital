# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** PSP-SAF-003  
**Title:** Medication Reconciliation during Patient Admission and Transfer  
**Domain:** Patient Safety  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Patient Safety Protocol (PSP) defines standard operating procedures for conducting comprehensive medication reconciliation at all care transitions to prevent medication errors, adverse drug events (ADEs), omitted doses, and unintentional duplicate therapies.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to admitting physicians, attending staff, clinical pharmacists, emergency department clinicians, and inpatient nursing staff across all admission, intra-facility transfer, and discharge care points.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Clinical Pharmacist / Admitting Nurse** is responsible for compiling the baseline Prior to Admission Medication History (PAMH) within the required timeframe upon patient intake.

### Clause 2.2: Prescribing Verification Lead
The **Admitting / Attending Physician** is responsible for reviewing the PAMH, comparing it against newly ordered inpatient medications, and documenting clinical reconciliation decisions.

---

## Section 3: Baseline Prior to Admission Medication History (PAMH)

### Clause 3.1: Data Sources for PAMH Compilation
Staff compiling the PAMH must verify medication information using at least two independent data sources:
1. Patient or caregiver interview.
2. Outpatient prescription container inspection.
3. Outpatient pharmacy dispensing records or prescription monitoring database.
4. Previous electronic health record discharge summaries.

### Clause 3.2: PAMH Completion Timeframe
The baseline PAMH must be completed, verified, and entered into the electronic health record within **24 hours** of inpatient admission (or within **4 hours** for high-risk medications such as anticoagulants, insulin, and immunosuppressive agents).

---

## Section 4: Care Transition Reconciliation Workflows

### Clause 4.1: Admission Reconciliation Sequence
When performing medication reconciliation upon inpatient admission, the prescribing clinician must execute the following sequential workflow:
1. Open the verified PAMH profile in the EHR system.
2. Review each home medication (prescription, over-the-counter, herbal supplements, and vitamins).
3. Assign an explicit status for each home medication: **Continue**, **Discontinue**, **Hold**, or **Modify Dose/Frequency**.
4. Screen newly prescribed inpatient medications for potential drug-drug interactions, therapeutic duplications, or known patient allergies.
5. Save and electronically sign the reconciled admission medication order set.

### Clause 4.2: Intra-Facility Transfer Reconciliation
During patient transfer between distinct care levels (e.g., ICU to General Medical-Surgical Ward):
1. The transferring physician must review all active ICU medication orders.
2. The physician must explicitly re-order or discontinue specialized infusions, sedation, and high-alert therapies prior to transfer execution.
3. The receiving nurse must verify the transfer reconciliation order set against active lines upon patient arrival.

---

## Section 5: Discrepancy Escalation and Audit Oversight

### Clause 5.1: Unintended Discrepancy Resolution
If a nurse or pharmacist identifies an unexplained discrepancy between home medications and active inpatient orders, they must contact the attending physician within **2 hours** to obtain clarification.

### Clause 5.2: Compliance Audits
The Pharmacy and Therapeutics (P&T) Safety Committee conducts monthly random sampling audits of at least 50 patient charts to evaluate medication reconciliation timeliness and accuracy.
