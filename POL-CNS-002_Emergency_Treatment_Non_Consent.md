# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** POL-CNS-002  
**Title:** Emergency Medical Treatment and Non-Consent Exceptions  
**Domain:** Consent Procedures  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Policy (POL) defines standard protocol and criteria for administering emergency medical interventions without explicit prior informed consent when a delay in treatment would result in severe permanent impairment or loss of life.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all emergency medicine physicians, trauma surgeons, critical care specialists, anesthesiologists, and emergency department clinical nurses across all emergency and acute care facilities.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Attending Emergency Physician / Trauma Surgeon** is responsible for evaluating patient decision-making capacity, declaring an emergency consent exception, and initiating life-saving medical care.

### Clause 2.2: Dual-Physician Concurrence
A **Second Independent Attending Physician** (or Chief Medical Officer delegate) is responsible for co-assessing the patient condition and co-signing the emergency consent exception documentation when feasible.

---

## Section 3: Statutory Emergency Exception Criteria

### Clause 3.1: Criteria for Presumed Consent
Emergency treatment without explicit written informed consent is authorized only when all of the following criteria are met simultaneously:
1. The patient suffers from an immediate life-threatening condition or threat of severe, irreversible organ dysfunction.
2. The patient lacks decision-making capacity due to unconsciousness, severe trauma, altered mental status, or acute delirium.
3. No legally authorized representative (surrogate, power of attorney, or immediate family) is immediately available to provide consent.
4. A reasonable person under similar circumstances would consent to the life-saving intervention.

### Clause 3.2: Known Prior Refusals
If a patient has a valid, documented Advance Directive, Living Will, or pre-existing legal refusal (e.g., Jehovah's Witness blood refusal card) known to the team, the known refusal overrides emergency presumed consent.

---

## Section 4: Emergency Non-Consent Execution Workflow

### Clause 4.1: Emergency Intervention Sequence
When encountering an incapacitated patient requiring urgent intervention, clinicians must execute the following sequential steps:
1. Attempt immediate verbal or telephonic contact with next of kin or designated legal surrogate.
2. If the surrogate cannot be reached without delaying life-saving treatment, initiate the "Emergency Exception Protocol."
3. Obtain verbal confirmation and second signature from a second independent attending physician.
4. Proceed immediately with necessary surgical, diagnostic, or therapeutic interventions.
5. Enter a detailed clinical note into the EHR titled "Emergency Consent Exception Form" within **2 hours** post-procedure, detailing the life threat and surrogate search efforts.
6. Continue attempts to contact family members and disclose performed emergency procedures as soon as possible.

---

## Section 5: Documentation and Administrative Audit

### Clause 5.1: EHR Documentation Mandate
The emergency consent note must document the specific life-threatening condition, why delay would increase risk, efforts made to reach surrogates, and signatures of two licensed attending physicians.

### Clause 5.2: Quality Review
All emergency non-consent interventions are audited monthly by the Hospital Risk Management and Medical Ethics Committee to verify compliance with statutory guidelines.
