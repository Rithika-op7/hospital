# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** SOP-OPS-005  
**Title:** Clinical Equipment Maintenance and Alarm Safety Management  
**Domain:** Hospital Operations  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) defines standards for preventive maintenance, pre-use safety inspections, and clinical alarm configuration to prevent equipment failures and eliminate alarm fatigue across patient care environments.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to biomedical engineering technicians, clinical care nurses, respiratory therapists, surgical technologists, and unit leaders utilizing or servicing medical devices across all inpatient and outpatient facilities.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Biomedical Engineering Department** is responsible for performing scheduled Preventive Maintenance (PM), tracking device inspection cycles, and certifying clinical equipment safety.

### Clause 2.2: Operational Monitoring Lead
The **Bedside Clinical Care Nurse** is responsible for verifying device operational integrity before patient attachment, setting customized alarm parameters, and responding promptly to active visual and audible alerts.

---

## Section 3: Equipment Tagging and Inspection Standards

### Clause 3.1: Preventive Maintenance (PM) Inspection Tags
1. Every clinical medical device (e.g., infusion pumps, patient monitors, ventilators, defibrillators) must display a valid, updated green **"Passed Biomedical Inspection" tag** indicating the next inspection due date.
2. Any device lacking a valid inspection tag or past its inspection due date must be removed from clinical service immediately.

### Clause 3.2: Defective Equipment Quarantine Tagging
When a medical device malfunctions or displays error codes, staff must immediately apply a red **"OUT OF SERVICE - DEFECTIVE" tag**, remove the device from patient areas, and log a service ticket with Biomedical Engineering.

---

## Section 4: Clinical Alarm Safety Management Workflow

### Clause 4.1: Alarm Fatigue Prevention Protocol
To prevent alarm fatigue and ensure critical physiological alarms receive rapid clinical attention:
1. Default alarm thresholds must be customized to fit individual patient clinical baseline parameters at the start of each shift.
2. Secondary or non-actionable notification volumes must be adjusted appropriately, while critical life-threatening alarm volume must remain audible above background room noise.
3. Electrodes and sensor cables must be replaced according to manufacturer guidelines to reduce false artifact alarms.

### Clause 4.2: Daily Equipment Pre-Use Verification Workflow
Prior to placing any clinical equipment on a patient, clinical staff must execute the following sequential workflow:
1. Visually inspect power cords, casings, and display panels for physical damage or wear.
2. Confirm presence of a current Biomedical PM green sticker.
3. Plug the device into an emergency backup power outlet (red wall receptacle).
4. Power on the device and verify successful completion of the automated self-test sequence.
5. Confirm alarm limits are enabled, set to patient-appropriate parameters, and set to active audibility mode.
6. Document equipment operational verification in the EHR setup checklist.

---

## Section 5: Adverse Incident Reporting and Device Sequestration

### Clause 5.1: Medical Device Incident Sequestration
If a medical device failure results in or contributes to patient injury, near-miss, or death, staff must immediately preserve all settings, disconnect the device from the patient, seal the unit without altering control knobs/drips, and notify Bio-Medical Engineering and Risk Management.

### Clause 5.2: Safe Medical Devices Act (SMDA) Compliance
The Hospital Safety Officer must report device-related serious injuries or deaths to the device manufacturer and relevant regulatory authorities within **10 business days**.
