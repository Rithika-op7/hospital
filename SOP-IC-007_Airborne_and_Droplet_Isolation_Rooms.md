# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** SOP-IC-007  
**Title:** Management of Airborne Infection Isolation Rooms (AIIR) and Droplet Isolation Units  
**Domain:** Infection Control  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) specifies engineering controls, door-monitored pressure differentials, airflow requirements, and room assignment workflows for Airborne Infection Isolation Rooms (AIIR) and Droplet Isolation Units to prevent airborne and droplet-borne pathogen transmission.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to facility engineers, infection control practitioners, nursing staff, and emergency department intake personnel across all inpatient care facilities.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Facilities Lead Engineer** is responsible for continuously monitoring negative pressure HVAC systems, verifying Air Changes Per Hour (ACH), and performing daily differential pressure sensor calibrations.

### Clause 2.2: Clinical Placement Lead
The **Infection Preventionist / Bed Placement Manager** is responsible for authorizing patient placement in AIIR units and enforcing isolation door status.

---

## Section 3: Engineering and Environmental Parameters

### Clause 3.1: AIIR Technical Standards
1. Airflow Differential: Continuous negative pressure relative to the surrounding hallways and anterooms, maintaining a minimum pressure differential of **-2.5 Pascals (-0.01 inches water gauge)**.
2. Air Change Rate: A minimum of **12 Air Changes Per Hour (ACH)** for newly constructed or renovated facilities (minimum 6 ACH for existing facilities).
3. Exhaust Handling: Total air exhaust must be vented directly to the outside away from air intake vents, or filtered through high-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filtration units before recirculation.

### Clause 3.2: Pressure Verification Procedures
Facilities staff and nursing personnel must verify negative pressure prior to patient occupancy and daily thereafter using both continuous electronic pressure monitors and visual tissue/ball-in-tube indicators.

---

## Section 4: Operational Workflow and Patient Isolation

### Clause 4.1: Patient Placement Sequence
When admitting or transferring a patient requiring airborne isolation, staff must execute the following sequential workflow:
1. Contact the Bed Placement Manager to identify an available, verified negative-pressure AIIR.
2. Confirm negative pressure status on the anteroom gauge before transferring the patient.
3. Place a surgical mask on the patient during transport to the isolation room.
4. Escort the patient into the AIIR and ensure both anteroom and main isolation doors remain completely closed.
5. Post designated "Airborne Isolation Precautions" signage outside the entry door.

### Clause 4.2: Door Management and Anteroom Rules
Anteroom doors and primary AIIR doors must never be open simultaneously. Anteroom doors must automatically close and latch after entry or exit.

---

## Section 5: Post-Discharge Clearance and HVAC Maintenance

### Clause 5.1: Room Clearance Time (Air Clearance)
After an airborne isolation patient is discharged, the room door must remain closed, and the room must remain vacant with negative pressure running for a specific clearance time based on ACH (e.g., **45 minutes at 12 ACH**) to allow 99.9% airborne contaminant removal prior to terminal cleaning.

### Clause 5.2: HEPA Filter Maintenance
Facilities engineers must inspect, test, and replace HEPA filters in AIIR exhaust systems every **6 months** or whenever pressure differential across the filter indicates saturation.
