# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** POL-CNS-003  
**Title:** Pediatric and Minors Informed Consent and Assent Protocols  
**Domain:** Consent Procedures  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Policy (POL) defines legal and ethical standards for obtaining parental or legal guardian consent, as well as pediatric patient assent, for surgical, interventional, and medical treatments administered to individuals under 18 years of age.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all pediatricians, pediatric surgeons, emergency physicians, pediatric nursing personnel, and registration staff across all inpatient pediatric units, pediatric ICUs, and outpatient pediatric clinics.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Attending Pediatric Physician / Surgeon** is responsible for conducting the informed consent discussion with parents or legal guardians and obtaining age-appropriate pediatric assent from the minor patient.

### Clause 2.2: Verification Lead
The **Pediatric Staff Nurse** is responsible for verifying that valid parental consent and pediatric assent forms are fully executed and placed in the medical record prior to procedure initiation.

---

## Section 3: Legal Requirements and Minor Assent Standards

### Clause 3.1: Parental/Guardian Consent Requirement
Except as provided by state law or emergency exceptions, all medical and surgical care for individuals under **18 years of age** requires written informed consent from at least one legally authorized parent or court-appointed legal guardian.

### Clause 3.2: Pediatric Assent Requirement
1. Pediatric patients aged **7 to 17 years** who possess sufficient cognitive development must be included in care discussions and asked to provide affirmative **Pediatric Assent**.
2. Assent is documented using a dedicated Pediatric Assent Form written in child-friendly language.
3. If a minor dissents to non-life-saving elective treatment, the clinical team must pause and conduct ethics consultation before proceeding.

---

## Section 4: Special Exceptions (Emancipated and Mature Minors)

### Clause 4.1: Autonomous Consent Criteria (No Parental Consent Required)
Minors under 18 may self-consent for medical and surgical treatment under the following legally defined exceptions:
1. **Emancipated Minors:** Minors who are legally married, active-duty military members, or declared emancipated by court decree.
2. **Reproductive and Sexual Health:** Confidential diagnosis and treatment for sexually transmitted infections (STIs), contraception, and pregnancy-related care.
3. **Substance Use & Mental Health:** Outpatient evaluation and treatment for substance dependency or acute mental health crisis as governed by statutory age limits (aged 14 and older).

---

## Section 5: Parental Consent Execution Workflow

### Clause 5.1: Execution Sequence for Pediatric Procedures
When preparing a pediatric patient for a medical or surgical procedure, staff must execute the following sequential workflow:
1. Confirm the legal authority of the accompanying adult (verify photo ID and guardianship/custody documentation).
2. The attending physician explains procedure details, risks, benefits, and alternatives to both parent and minor.
3. Obtain signature of the parent/guardian on the Informed Consent Form.
4. Obtain signature or verbal affirmation of the minor (if aged 7-17) on the Pediatric Assent Form.
5. The staff nurse witnesses both signatures and verifies documentation in the EHR.

### Clause 5.2: Parental Disagreement Resolution
In non-emergent situations where joint legal guardians disagree on a treatment plan, care must be deferred until legal guardianship consensus is resolved or court intervention is obtained.
