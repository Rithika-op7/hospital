# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** MAN-CMP-002  
**Title:** HIPAA Privacy Policy and Data Safeguards  
**Domain:** Compliance Manual  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Manual (MAN) establishes privacy standards, administrative safeguards, and technical security protocols for protecting Protected Health Information (PHI) in compliance with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and applicable privacy regulations.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all healthcare providers, administrative personnel, IT specialists, contractors, business associates, and volunteers accessing, handling, or processing physical or electronic PHI (ePHI).

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Hospital Privacy Officer (HPO)** is responsible for drafting privacy protocols, managing patient privacy rights, evaluating potential data disclosures, and reporting breaches to regulatory bodies.

### Clause 2.2: Technical Safeguard Lead
The **Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)** is responsible for implementing technical security controls, electronic access permissions, encryption standards, and network audit logs.

---

## Section 3: Protected Health Information (PHI) and Minimum Necessary Standard

### Clause 3.1: Definition of PHI
PHI encompasses any individually identifiable health information held or transmitted by the hospital, including physical charts, electronic medical records, billing data, diagnostic images, and verbal care discussions.

### Clause 3.2: Minimum Necessary Rule
1. Personnel must access, use, or disclose only the **minimum amount of PHI necessary** to accomplish the intended clinical or administrative purpose.
2. Direct clinical care providers are exempt from the minimum necessary rule when actively evaluating or treating a patient.

---

## Section 4: Data Safeguards and Breach Incident Response Workflow

### Clause 4.1: Technical and Physical Safeguard Requirements
1. **Workstation Security:** Computers and clinical terminals must lock automatically after **5 minutes of inactivity**. Users must never share login credentials.
2. **Physical Security:** Paper charts and records must be locked in designated record storage rooms or secure cabinets when unattended.
3. **Data Encryption:** All portable storage devices, laptops, and mobile messaging tools containing ePHI must be protected by end-to-end 256-bit AES encryption.

### Clause 4.2: Breach Identification and Escalation Workflow
When an unauthorized access, disclosure, or theft of PHI is suspected, staff must execute the following sequential workflow:
1. Disconnect compromised devices from the local network and notify the IT Helpdesk and Privacy Office immediately.
2. The Privacy Officer logs the incident and initiates a four-factor risk assessment to evaluate:
   - Nature and extent of PHI involved.
   - Unauthorized person who used or received the info.
   - Whether PHI was actually viewed or acquired.
   - Extent to which risk has been mitigated.
3. If the risk assessment confirms a reportable breach, notify affected individuals in writing without unreasonable delay and no later than **60 calendar days** following discovery.
4. If a breach affects **500 or more individuals**, notify the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) and prominent media outlets concurrently.

---

## Section 5: Patient Rights and Audit Logging

### Clause 5.1: Patient Privacy Rights
Patients maintain statutory rights to request access to their medical records, request corrections to erroneous entries, receive an accounting of disclosures, and request restrictions on non-routine data sharing.

### Clause 5.2: EHR Access Auditing
The CISO conducts automated, continuous audit tracking of all EHR record access. Any employee accessing records of family members, celebrities, co-workers, or patients outside their direct care scope is flagged for immediate disciplinary review.
