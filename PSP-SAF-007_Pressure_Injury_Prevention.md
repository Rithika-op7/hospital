# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** PSP-SAF-007  
**Title:** Pressure Injury Prevention and Staging Protocol  
**Domain:** Patient Safety  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Patient Safety Protocol (PSP) defines mandatory clinical procedures for assessing skin integrity, identifying risk factors, staging existing wounds, and executing preventive skin care interventions to eliminate hospital-acquired pressure injuries (HAPIs).

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all clinical nursing staff, wound care specialists, physical therapists, intensive care staff, and surgical recovery nurses caring for adult and pediatric inpatients.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Primary Staff Nurse** assigned to the patient is responsible for performing skin risk assessments, documenting baseline skin condition, and carrying out mandatory turning and positioning schedules.

### Clause 2.2: Specialist Lead
The **Wound, Ostomy, and Continence (WOC) Nurse Specialist** is responsible for evaluating Stage 3, Stage 4, unstageable, and deep tissue pressure injuries, and formulating specialized wound treatment plans.

---

## Section 3: Risk Assessment Frequency and Tools

### Clause 3.1: Validated Risk Assessment Tool
Primary staff must evaluate pressure injury risk using the validated **Braden Scale for Predicting Pressure Sore Risk** (evaluating sensory perception, moisture, activity, mobility, nutrition, and friction/shear).

### Clause 3.2: Assessment Schedule
Skin inspection and Braden Scale scoring must be documented in the EHR at the following mandatory intervals:
1. Within **4 hours** of inpatient admission or emergency department intake.
2. At least once daily (**every 24 hours**) for general inpatient ward units.
3. Every shift (**every 12 hours**) for intensive care unit (ICU) and high-acuity patients.
4. Following any major surgical procedure lasting > 4 hours.

---

## Section 4: Prevention Intervention Workflow (Braden Score ≤ 18)

### Clause 4.1: Moderate to Severe Risk Interventions
When a patient scores **18 or lower** on the Braden Scale (indicating pressure injury risk), staff must execute the following sequential intervention workflow:
1. Place a pressure-redistributing dynamic air mattress or specialized foam overlay on the patient's bed.
2. Establish a mandatory **2-hour repositioning schedule** for bedbound patients and a 1-hour repositioning schedule for chair-bound patients.
3. Apply prophylactic silicone foam dressings to high-risk bony prominences (sacrum, heels).
4. Float heels off the bed surface using specialized heel offloading boots or pillows placed under the lower legs.
5. Use friction-reducing slide sheets and maintain the head of the bed at or below **30 degrees** (unless contraindicated) to minimize shear forces.
6. Apply barrier creams following each incontinent episode to protect skin from moisture-associated skin damage (MASD).

---

## Section 5: Injury Staging and Mandatory Escalation

### Clause 5.1: Pressure Injury Staging Definitions
1. Stage 1: Non-blanchable erythema of intact skin.
2. Stage 2: Partial-thickness skin loss with exposed dermis (viable, pink/red wound bed).
3. Stage 3: Full-thickness skin loss with visible adipose tissue (subcutaneous fat).
4. Stage 4: Full-thickness skin and tissue loss with exposed bone, tendon, ligament, or muscle.
5. Unstageable: Full-thickness loss where extent of damage cannot be confirmed due to slough or eschar.

### Clause 5.2: Escalation Protocol for New HAPIs
If a new Stage 2 or greater hospital-acquired pressure injury is discovered, the staff nurse must document the wound dimensions, notify the attending physician and WOC Nurse within **12 hours**, and submit a Safety Incident Report.
