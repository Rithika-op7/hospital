# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** SOP-OPS-003  
**Title:** Patient Transfer and Intra-Facility Transport Safety  
**Domain:** Hospital Operations  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) defines standardized safety requirements, clinical handoff workflows, and equipment monitoring protocols for transporting inpatients between departments, diagnostic suites, or intensive care units within the facility.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all registered nurses, respiratory therapists, transport technicians, radiology staff, and attending physicians involved in intra-facility patient movement.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Sending Primary Nurse** is responsible for conducting the pre-transport stability assessment, preparing portable monitoring equipment, and performing structured handoff communication with the receiving unit.

### Clause 2.2: Escort Accompaniment Lead
The **Transport Escort (RN or Respiratory Therapist)** is responsible for continuously monitoring vital signs, managing active line infusions, and maintaining airway stability during transit.

---

## Section 3: Transport Escalation Tiers and Personnel Requirements

### Clause 3.1: Minimum Escort Requirements
1. **Low Risk (Unmonitored):** Transport Technician or Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA) for stable patients requiring routine diagnostic imaging.
2. **Moderate Risk (Telemetry / IV Therapy):** Registered Nurse (RN) required for patients with active telemetry, continuous IV drips, or high risk of acute deterioration.
3. **High Risk (ICU / Mechanical Ventilation):** Critical Care RN and Respiratory Therapist (RT) required for intubated patients, patients receiving continuous vasoactive infusions, or patients with invasive arterial monitoring.

---

## Section 4: Pre-Transport Assessment and Handoff Workflow

### Clause 4.1: Sequential Intra-Facility Transport Workflow
Prior to moving any patient out of a clinical care unit, staff must execute the following sequential workflow:
1. Re-assess patient vital signs and clinical stability within **15 minutes** prior to departure.
2. Verify that portable oxygen tanks have at least **500 psi** of pressure remaining (or sufficient volume for twice the estimated transport time).
3. Secure all IV lines, urinary drainage bags, and chest tubes below the level of the patient's torso.
4. Perform verbal **SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation)** handoff with the receiving department clinician via telephone or secure direct contact.
5. Place the patient on a portable battery-powered cardiac monitor/defibrillator for all telemetry or ICU transfers.
6. Transport the patient using the primary bed or high-sided stretcher with all side rails locked in the upright position.
7. Upon arrival at the receiving unit, conduct bedside handoff and re-verify vital signs before transferring primary care responsibility.

---

## Section 5: Equipment Failure and Emergency Escalation

### Clause 5.1: Transit Clinical Deterioration Protocol
If a patient experiences severe acute clinical deterioration, cardiac arrest, or accidental extubation during transit in a hallway or elevator, the escort team must immediately stop, activate the nearest wall emergency alarm or call ext. 5555 for an emergency response, and initiate basic resuscitation.

### Clause 5.2: Transport Log Documentation
The sending nurse must log the departure time, escort names, baseline vitals, and arrival handoff completion in the patient's electronic health record within **1 hour** post-transport.
