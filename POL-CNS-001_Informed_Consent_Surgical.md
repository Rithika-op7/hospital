# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** POL-CNS-001  
**Title:** Informed Consent Requirements for Surgical and Invasive Procedures  
**Domain:** Consent Procedures  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Policy (POL) defines mandatory legal and ethical standards for obtaining, documenting, and verifying voluntary informed consent prior to performing surgical, diagnostic, or therapeutic invasive procedures.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all attending physicians, surgeons, interventional specialists, anesthesiologists, and clinical nursing personnel across all outpatient, inpatient, and surgical units.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Attending Physician / Operating Surgeon** is legally and professionally responsible for conducting the informed consent discussion, explaining risks and benefits, and obtaining the signed consent form.

### Clause 2.2: Nurse Witness Role
The **Pre-Operative / Circulating Nurse** is responsible for witnessing the signing of the consent form, verifying that the document is complete and active, and confirming the patient's understanding prior to procedure initiation.

---

## Section 3: Elements of Valid Informed Consent

### Clause 3.1: Mandatory Discussion Components
The informed consent conversation must be conducted in language understandable to the patient and must explicitly cover:
1. Diagnosis and nature of the proposed procedure.
2. Expected clinical benefits and likelihood of success.
3. Material risks, potential complications, and alternative treatments (including no treatment).
4. Names of primary attending physicians or surgeons performing the procedure.

### Clause 3.2: Validity Period
A signed informed consent form remains valid for a maximum of **30 calendar days** from the date of signature, provided there is no change in the patient's clinical condition or procedure plan.

---

## Section 4: Informed Consent Execution Workflow

### Clause 4.1: Standard Consent Workflow
Prior to transferring a patient to the procedure area, staff must execute the following sequential workflow:
1. The attending physician conducts the consent discussion with the patient or legal surrogate.
2. Provide the patient or surrogate the opportunity to ask clarifying questions.
3. Obtain the physical or electronic signature of the patient or legally authorized representative (LAR).
4. The witness signs and dates the consent form, confirming the signature was executed voluntarily.
5. Upload or place the completed consent form into the active patient electronic medical record (EMR).
6. The perioperative nurse verifies that the consent form matches the scheduled procedure during pre-op intake.

### Clause 4.2: Special Language and Disability Support
If the patient is non-English speaking or sensory-impaired, a qualified medical interpreter (in-person or remote video) must assist during the discussion. The interpreter's name and ID number must be recorded on the consent document.

---

## Section 5: Revocation and Consent Revocation Workflow

### Clause 5.1: Right to Revoke
A patient retains the right to revoke informed consent at any point prior to the administration of anesthesia or initiation of the procedure.

### Clause 5.2: Revocation Handling
If a patient revokes consent, all procedure preparations must stop immediately, the attending physician must be notified, and the revocation must be documented in the patient chart.
