# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** PSP-SAF-002  
**Title:** Fall Risk Assessment and High-Risk Patient Management  
**Domain:** Patient Safety  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Patient Safety Protocol (PSP) defines standard procedures for identifying adult and pediatric inpatients at risk for accidental falls and implementing universal and high-risk fall prevention interventions to eliminate patient injuries.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This protocol applies to all clinical personnel, including registered nurses, nursing assistants, physical therapists, occupational therapists, and transport staff across all inpatient acute care and emergency units.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Primary Care Nurse** is responsible for performing mandatory fall risk screenings upon admission, executing risk-level reassessments every shift, and placing visual indicators for high-risk patients.

### Clause 2.2: Assistive Team Support
The **Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA)** is responsible for assisting high-risk patients with toileting, responding immediately to bed alarms, and ensuring mobility aids remain within patient reach.

---

## Section 3: Fall Risk Screening Schedule and Tools

### Clause 3.1: Validated Assessment Tools
1. Adult Inpatients: Primary staff must assess fall risk using the validated **Morse Fall Scale (MFS)**.
2. Pediatric Inpatients: Primary staff must assess fall risk using the validated **Humpty Dumpty Fall Scale**.

### Clause 3.2: Assessment Frequency
Fall risk screening must be conducted and documented in the Electronic Health Record (EHR) at the following mandatory intervals:
1. Within **2 hours** of patient admission to an inpatient unit.
2. At the beginning of every nursing shift (**every 12 hours**).
3. Immediately following any patient transfer between units.
4. Immediately following any clinical change in condition or medication adjustment (e.g., administration of sedatives or antihypertensives).
5. Post-fall event evaluation.

---

## Section 4: Universal and High-Risk Prevention Interventions

### Clause 4.1: Universal Fall Precautions (All Patients)
Universal fall precautions must be maintained for all inpatients regardless of risk score:
1. Maintain bed in lowest position with wheels locked.
2. Keep call bell and personal items within direct reach of the patient.
3. Ensure night lights are active in patient rooms and bathrooms.
4. Keep walkways clear of clutter and IV tubing hazards.
5. Provide non-skid socks for patient ambulation.

### Clause 4.2: High-Risk Intervention Workflow (Morse Score ≥ 45)
When a patient scores **45 or higher** on the Morse Fall Scale (High Risk), staff must execute the following sequential intervention workflow:
1. Place a yellow **"High Fall Risk" wristband** on the patient.
2. Apply a visual yellow fall risk indicator magnet/sign on the doorframe of the patient's room.
3. Activate an operational bed or chair exit alarm set to immediate alert mode.
4. Establish a scheduled **"Intentioned Hourly Rounding"** protocol (assessing pain, potty, positioning, and possessions every 60 minutes).
5. Ensure 2-person assistance or physical therapy consultation for all patient transfers and ambulation.

---

## Section 5: Post-Fall Emergency Management and Reporting

### Clause 5.1: Post-Fall Immediate Care
If a patient fall occurs, staff must immediately assess the patient for injury prior to moving them, obtain vital signs, perform a baseline neurological assessment, and notify the attending physician.

### Clause 5.2: Mandatory Event Escalation
The charge nurse must complete an online Safety Incident Report within **24 hours** of the fall event, perform a post-fall huddle with unit staff, and log details into the hospital root cause database.
