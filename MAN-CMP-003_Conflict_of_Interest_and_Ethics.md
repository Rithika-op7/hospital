# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** MAN-CMP-003  
**Title:** Conflict of Interest and Professional Ethics Policy  
**Domain:** Compliance Manual  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Manual (MAN) defines organizational standards for identifying, disclosing, evaluating, and managing real, potential, or perceived conflicts of interest (COI) to maintain professional integrity, objective clinical decision-making, and ethical business practices.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all board members, executive officers, physicians, department heads, procurement staff, research investigators, and clinical employees across all hospital entities and administrative divisions.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Corporate Ethics & Compliance Committee** is responsible for reviewing annual conflict disclosures, evaluating potential financial or professional conflicts, and issuing mandatory conflict management plans.

### Clause 2.2: Individual Disclosure Obligation
All **Covered Persons** (physicians, executives, procurement officers, researchers) are individually responsible for proactively disclosing outside financial interests, secondary employment, and industry relationships prior to engaging in decision-making.

---

## Section 3: Prohibited Financial Conflicts and Gift Thresholds

### Clause 3.1: Prohibited Activities
1. **Self-Dealing:** Awarding hospital procurement contracts, vendor agreements, or consulting work to entities owned by an employee or their immediate family members without formal competitive bidding and prior committee approval.
2. **Prescription Steering:** Accepting compensation, consulting fees, or kickbacks from pharmaceutical or medical device manufacturers in exchange for favoring specific drugs, implants, or supplies on the hospital formulary.

### Clause 3.2: Vendor Gifts and Interaction Thresholds
1. Covered persons must not accept personal gifts, gratuities, entertainment, or luxury travel from vendor representatives regardless of value.
2. Modest food or refreshments provided in connection with certified educational events may be accepted only if the total per-person value does not exceed **$25 per event** (up to an annual cap of **$100** per vendor).

---

## Section 4: Conflict Disclosure and Management Workflow

### Clause 4.1: Conflict Evaluation and Escalation Sequence
When a potential conflict of interest arises, staff must execute the following sequential workflow:
1. Complete and submit the electronic **Conflict of Interest Disclosure Form** via the Compliance Portal annually or within **30 calendar days** of acquiring a new reportable interest.
2. The Compliance Office reviews the submission and classifies the disclosure as: Low Risk, Manageable Risk, or Unmanageable/Prohibited.
3. For Manageable Risk disclosures, the Corporate Ethics & Compliance Committee drafts a formal **Conflict Management Plan (CMP)** detailing required recusal boundaries.
4. The Covered Person signs the CMP and agrees to recuse themselves from purchasing decisions, contract negotiations, or patient selection relevant to the disclosed interest.
5. The Compliance Officer conducts annual reviews to verify ongoing adherence to the CMP.

---

## Section 5: Non-Disclosure Sanctions and Sunshine Act Compliance

### Clause 5.1: Failure to Disclose Sanctions
Falsification, deliberate omission, or failure to submit required COI disclosures constitutes serious non-compliance resulting in revocation of clinical privileges, removal from committee posts, or termination of employment.

### Clause 5.2: Open Payments (Sunshine Act) Reconciliation
The Compliance Office audits payments reported by pharmaceutical and medical device manufacturers under federal Physician Payments Sunshine Act databases to verify consistency with internal physician disclosure records.
