# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** PSP-SAF-005  
**Title:** Critical Laboratory and Diagnostic Test Result Escalation  
**Domain:** Patient Safety  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Patient Safety Protocol (PSP) defines mandatory communication channels and immediate escalation timelines for reporting critical laboratory, radiological, and diagnostic test results that indicate a life-threatening condition requiring immediate medical intervention.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all diagnostic laboratory personnel, radiology technologists, radiologists, clinical nursing staff, attending physicians, and mid-level providers across all inpatient and outpatient care settings.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Diagnostic Laboratory Technician / Radiologist** is responsible for identifying a critical test result, initiating the direct verbal communication chain, and tracking escalation timers.

### Clause 2.2: Receiving Care Lead
The **Primary Staff Nurse / Attending Physician** receiving the critical report is responsible for performing a mandatory read-back, acknowledging the result in the EHR, and initiating clinical interventions.

---

## Section 3: Critical Result Identification and Standards

### Clause 3.1: Critical Value Definition
A critical result is defined as a test value or diagnostic finding that falls significantly outside the normal reference range, representing an immediate life-threatening risk to the patient if unaddressed (e.g., Potassium < 2.5 or > 6.0 mEq/L, Hemoglobin < 6.0 g/dL, critical CT brain hemorrhage).

### Clause 3.2: Verbal Communication Requirement
Critical results must be communicated directly via direct verbal contact (in person or via telephone). Electronic notifications or voicemails alone do not satisfy critical result reporting requirements.

---

## Section 4: Reporting Timelines and Communication Workflow

### Clause 4.1: Reporting Timelines
1. Laboratory and Diagnostic Staff must verbally report critical test results to the responsible clinical caregiver within **60 minutes** of result verification.
2. For high-acuity units (ICU, Emergency Department, Operating Room), verbal reporting must occur within **15 minutes** of result verification.

### Clause 4.2: Sequential Communication Workflow
When a critical result is verified, diagnostic staff must execute the following sequential workflow:
1. Contact the primary care nurse or attending physician assigned to the patient.
2. State clearly: "This is a Critical Test Result notification for [Patient Name, DOB]."
3. Provide the exact diagnostic test name, parameter, and numerical/qualitative result.
4. Require the receiving clinician to perform a mandatory **"Read-Back"** (reading the patient name, test, and exact numerical value back to the reporter).
5. Document in the laboratory/radiology information system: the full name of the receiving clinician, time of verbal contact, read-back confirmation, and technician ID.

---

## Section 5: Escalation Protocol for Unreachable Providers

### Clause 5.1: Tiered Escalation Pathway
If the primary care nurse or ordering physician cannot be reached within **15 minutes** of initial contact attempts, diagnostic staff must escalate through the following chain:
1. Contact the Unit Charge Nurse.
2. If unreachable within 10 minutes, contact the On-Call Resident or Medical Officer.
3. If unreachable within 10 minutes, contact the Chief Medical Officer (CMO) or Department Chair.

### Clause 5.2: Documentation of Escalation
All failed communication attempts, timestamps, and escalation steps must be logged in the electronic audit trail for patient safety compliance review.
