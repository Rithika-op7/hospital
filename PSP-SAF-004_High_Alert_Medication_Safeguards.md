# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** PSP-SAF-004  
**Title:** High-Alert Medication Administration and Double-Check Safeguards  
**Domain:** Patient Patient Safety  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Patient Safety Protocol (PSP) defines mandatory safety protocols for prescribing, storing, preparing, and administering High-Alert Medications (HAMs) to prevent severe medication errors and catastrophic patient harm.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This protocol applies to all registered nurses, clinical pharmacists, attending physicians, anesthesiologists, and critical care staff administering medications across all inpatient care units, emergency departments, and surgical suites.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Administering Registered Nurse** is responsible for initiating and leading the independent double-check verification process prior to introducing any high-alert medication to a patient.

### Clause 2.2: Verifying Role
A **Second Qualified Clinician (Registered Nurse, Clinical Pharmacist, or Physician)** is responsible for independently calculating, verifying, and signing off on the medication parameters prior to administration.

---

## Section 3: High-Alert Medication Classification and Storage

### Clause 3.1: Designated High-Alert Categories
High-alert medications subject to this protocol include:
1. Intravenous Anticoagulants (e.g., Unfractionated Heparin, Alteplase).
2. Subcutaneous and Intravenous Insulin preparations.
3. Concentrated Electrolytes (e.g., Potassium Chloride ≥2 mEq/mL, Sodium Chloride >0.9%).
4. Continuous Opioid Infusions and Patient-Controlled Analgesia (PCA) pumps.
5. Neuromuscular Blocking Agents (e.g., Succinylcholine, Rocuronium).
6. Chemotherapy and Cytotoxic Agents.

### Clause 3.2: Storage and Labeling Requirements
1. Concentrated electrolytes must never be stored in general inpatient ward patient care areas; they must be restricted to Pharmacy or Intensive Care Units.
2. High-alert medications stored in Automated Dispensing Cabinets (ADCs) must be placed in segregated, high-risk lockboxes with auxiliary warning labels ("HIGH ALERT - DOUBLE CHECK REQUIRED").

---

## Section 4: Mandatory Independent Double-Check Procedure

### Clause 4.1: Independent Verification Concept
An independent double-check requires the second qualified clinician to verify each step separately without relying on the primary nurse stating what the value or dose should be.

### Clause 4.2: Sequential Verification Workflow
Prior to administering any High-Alert Medication, the two clinicians must complete the following sequential workflow:
1. **Order Verification:** Cross-check the electronic medication administration record (eMAR) against the original physician order.
2. **Product Verification:** Compare the physical medication vial or IV bag label against the eMAR, verifying correct drug name, concentration, and expiration date.
3. **Calculation Verification:** Independently calculate the total dose, volume, infusion rate (mL/hr), and concentration without consulting the other clinician's notes.
4. **Patient Identification:** Verify patient identity using two unique identifiers (Name and DOB) against the wristband and eMAR.
5. **Pump Programming Verification:** For IV infusions, verify smart infusion pump programming parameters (Dose Error Reduction System / drug library selection, dose, and rate) at the bedside.
6. **Dual EHR Documentation:** Both clinicians must enter their credentials into the eMAR to record the successful dual sign-off before administration starts.

---

## Section 5: Pump Overrides and Emergency Escalation

### Clause 5.1: Smart Pump Guardrail Overrides
Soft limit overrides on smart pumps must be documented in the eMAR with a clinical justification. Hard limit overrides are strictly prohibited.

### Clause 5.2: Error Escalation Protocol
If the two verifying clinicians identify a calculation mismatch or pump programming discrepancy, administration must be halted immediately, and the clinical pharmacist must be contacted to resolve the discrepancy before proceeding.
