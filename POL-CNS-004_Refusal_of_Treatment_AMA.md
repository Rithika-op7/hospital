# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** POL-CNS-004  
**Title:** Refusal of Treatment and Discharge Against Medical Advice (AMA)  
**Domain:** Consent Procedures  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Policy (POL) defines operational procedures, capacity assessment protocols, and legal documentation requirements for managing adult patients who exercise their autonomous right to refuse recommended medical treatment or request Discharge Against Medical Advice (AMA).

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all attending physicians, resident physicians, hospitalists, registered nurses, clinical social workers, and risk management staff across all emergency, inpatient, and surgical units.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Attending Physician / Treating Hospitalist** is responsible for assessing the patient's decision-making capacity, explaining the specific clinical risks of treatment refusal or premature discharge, and completing the formal AMA discussion.

### Clause 2.2: Supportive Nursing and Social Work Lead
The **Primary Care Nurse** and **Clinical Social Worker** are responsible for witnessing the refusal documentation, coordinating safe discharge discharge instructions, and ensuring outpatient prescription access.

---

## Section 3: Assessment of Decision-Making Capacity

### Clause 3.1: Capacity Assessment Requirement
Before accepting a refusal of life-sustaining treatment or executing an AMA discharge, the attending physician must evaluate and document that the patient possesses medical **Decision-Making Capacity**.

### Clause 3.2: Elements of Decision-Making Capacity
To demonstrate capacity, the patient must be able to:
1. Receive and understand relevant clinical information regarding their diagnosis.
2. Appreciate the nature of their medical condition and the risks of refusing treatment (including permanent disability or death).
3. Reason rationally through treatment options and state a clear, un-coerced decision.
4. Communicate their choice consistently to the care team.

---

## Section 4: Treatment Refusal and AMA Execution Workflow

### Clause 4.1: AMA Discharge Execution Sequence
When a competent patient requests to discontinue care or leave the facility against medical advice, staff must execute the following sequential workflow:
1. Notify the attending physician immediately upon patient request to leave.
2. The attending physician conducts an in-person bedside consultation to discuss underlying patient concerns (e.g., pain, anxiety, financial/family issues) and explore compromise management options.
3. If the patient maintains their decision to leave, the physician must explicitly warn the patient of specific medical risks, potential complications, permanent injury, or mortality.
4. Present the formal **"Discharge Against Medical Advice (AMA) Form"** for patient signature.
5. If the patient signed or verbally refused to sign, the nurse and physician must co-sign as witnesses.
6. Provide the patient with standard discharge instructions, appropriate outpatient prescriptions (unless clinically unsafe), and follow-up clinic contact information.
7. Document the full capacity assessment, risk discussion details, and witness names in the EHR.

### Clause 4.2: Involuntary Hold Exception
If a patient lacking capacity attempts to leave the hospital, an AMA discharge must not be executed. The physician must initiate emergency safety measures or a temporary involuntary hold in accordance with state mental health laws.

---

## Section 5: Preserving Right to Follow-up Care

### Clause 5.1: Non-Punitive Discharge Policy
Discharging a patient AMA does not void the hospital's obligation to provide emergency care in the future. Staff are strictly prohibited from refusing future emergency intake or withholding post-discharge prescriptions solely because a patient executed an AMA discharge.

### Clause 5.2: Risk Management Escalation
All AMA discharges involving high-risk clinical conditions (e.g., acute myocardial infarction, active sepsis, acute surgical abdomen) must be flagged for review by the Hospital Risk Management Office within **24 hours**.
