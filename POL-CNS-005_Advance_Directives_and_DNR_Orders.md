# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** POL-CNS-005  
**Title:** Advance Directives and Do Not Resuscitate (DNR) Orders  
**Domain:** Consent Procedures  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Policy (POL) specifies procedures for identifying, validating, documenting, and honoring patient Advance Directives, Durable Powers of Attorney for Healthcare (DPOAHC), and Do Not Resuscitate (DNR) / Medical Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment (MOLST) orders.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all attending physicians, hospitalists, emergency room providers, critical care staff, inpatient nurses, social workers, and admissions staff across all inpatient and outpatient departments.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Attending Physician** is responsible for conducting goals-of-care discussions with the patient or legal surrogate, confirming the validity of Advance Directives, writing specific DNR/POLST orders in the EHR, and communicating care limits to the clinical team.

### Clause 2.2: Administrative Verification Lead
The **Admissions / Nursing Lead** is responsible for screening all admitted patients for Advance Directives, securing copies of legal documents, and flagging DNR status visually in the EHR and bedside.

---

## Section 3: Document Types and Verification Standards

### Clause 3.1: Valid Advance Directive Documents
The hospital recognizes the following legal end-of-life directives:
1. **Living Will:** Written instructions regarding specific end-of-life medical treatments desired or refused.
2. **Durable Power of Attorney for Healthcare (DPOAHC):** Legal appointment of a healthcare proxy to make decisions if the patient loses capacity.
3. **Do Not Resuscitate (DNR) Order:** A specific medical order issued by an attending physician directing that cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) be withheld in the event of cardiac or respiratory arrest.

### Clause 3.2: Verification and EHR Flagging
Upon receipt of an Advance Directive or surrogate designation document, administrative or clinical staff must verify legal signatures/notarization and flag the patient's record as "Advance Directive on File" within **12 hours** of admission.

---

## Section 4: Code Status Discussion and DNR Order Execution Workflow

### Clause 4.1: Standard DNR Execution Sequence
When establishing or updating a patient's resuscitation status, clinicians must execute the following sequential workflow:
1. Review existing Advance Directives or legal surrogate documentation in the chart.
2. Conduct a goals-of-care discussion with the competent patient or the designated DPOAHC surrogate.
3. Clarify specific intervention preferences: Full Code, DNR-CCA (Comfort Care Arrest - full care until arrest), or DNR-CC (Comfort Care Only - palliative measures only).
4. The attending physician enters the formal DNR order into the EHR order entry system.
5. Place a standard purple **"DNR" identification band** on the patient's wrist.
6. Communicate the code status to all care team members during shift handoff.

### Clause 4.2: Perioperative Suspension of DNR Orders
DNR orders are not automatically canceled during surgical procedures. Prior to surgery, the attending surgeon, anesthesiologist, and patient/surrogate must hold a explicit discussion to decide whether to maintain, modify, or temporarily suspend the DNR order during the perioperative window.

---

## Section 5: Dispute Resolution and Ethics Consultation

### Clause 5.1: Surrogate Conflicts
If family members or co-surrogates disagree on the interpretation of an Advance Directive or DNR order, the attending physician must maintain existing supportive care while initiating an urgent Bioethics Consultation.

### Clause 5.2: Revocation Protocol
A patient with decision-making capacity may revoke their Advance Directive or DNR status at any time by verbal or written declaration. The primary nurse must immediately notify the physician to cancel the DNR order in the EHR.
