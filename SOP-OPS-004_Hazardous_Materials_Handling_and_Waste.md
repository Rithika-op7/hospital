# HOSPITAL OPERATIONAL POLICY

**Document ID:** SOP-OPS-004  
**Title:** Hazardous Materials Handling and Waste Disposal  
**Domain:** Hospital Operations  

---

## Section 1: Purpose & Scope

### Clause 1.1: Objective
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) defines mandatory containment, handling, storage, and disposal protocols for biohazardous waste, chemical agents, cytotoxic pharmaceuticals, and radioactive materials to protect hospital personnel and ensure compliance with environmental safety regulations.

### Clause 1.2: Applicability
This policy applies to all environmental services (EVS) staff, laboratory technicians, clinical nurses, pharmacists, nuclear medicine specialists, and facility engineers across all hospital care settings and diagnostic laboratories.

---

## Section 2: Roles and Responsibilities

### Clause 2.1: Primary Responsible Role
The **Environmental Services (EVS) Safety Lead / Lab Safety Officer** is responsible for overseeing hazardous waste collection schedules, inspect storage receptacles, and maintaining Safety Data Sheets (SDS).

### Clause 2.2: Generating Clinician Role
The **Clinical Staff / Technician** generating the waste is responsible for correctly segregating and disposing of hazardous materials at the immediate point of origin.

---

## Section 3: Waste Segregation Categories and Color Standards

### Clause 3.1: Waste Classification and Container Standards
1. **Regulated Medical Waste (Biohazard):** Contaminated dressings, blood products, and tissue placed in leak-proof **Red Biohazard Bags**.
2. **Sharps Waste:** Needles, scalpels, lancets, and broken glass vials placed directly into puncture-resistant, rigid **Red/Yellow Sharps Containers**.
3. **Trace Chemotherapy Waste:** Empty vials, IV bags, and gloves used in cytotoxic administration placed in rigid **Yellow Trace Waste Bins**.
4. **Pharmaceutical Waste:** Expired or partially used non-controlled medications placed in **Blue Pharmaceutical Containers**.
5. **Radioactive Waste:** Isotope containers and radio-contaminated materials managed in lead-shielded **Radioactive Storage Bins**.

---

## Section 4: Spill Response and Handling Workflow

### Clause 4.1: Chemical/Biohazardous Spill Response Sequence
When a hazardous chemical or biohazardous fluid spill occurs, staff must execute the following sequential workflow:
1. Evacuate the immediate area and restrict access to prevent exposure.
2. Consult the specific **Safety Data Sheet (SDS)** located in the electronic portal or binder.
3. Don appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) including chemical-resistant gloves, eye protection/face shield, and fluid-resistant apron.
4. Obtain the standardized **Hazardous Materials Spill Kit** from the designated clean utility room.
5. Apply absorbent spill-lock powder or containment booms around the perimeter of the liquid spill.
6. Scoop up absorbed material using non-sparking tools and place into designated hazardous disposal bags.
7. Decontaminate the affected surface using an approved high-level disinfectant solution (e.g., 1:10 sodium hypochlorite dilution).
8. Remove PPE in the proper order and place contaminated gear inside the biohazard waste bag.
9. Notify the EVS Safety Lead and submit an Environmental Incident Report within **4 hours**.

---

## Section 5: Storage and Manifest Tracking

### Clause 5.1: Central Storage Containment
Hazardous waste storage rooms must remain locked, temperature-controlled, clearly labeled with biohazard warnings, and accessible only to authorized personnel.

### Clause 5.2: Disposal Manifest Audits
All hazardous waste removed by licensed external disposal contractors must be logged with signed Hazardous Waste Manifests maintained by the Facilities Department for a minimum of **3 years**.
